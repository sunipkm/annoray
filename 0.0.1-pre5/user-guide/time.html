Dates, times and time zones#

A date-time in annoray is an instant: a point on the UTC time line, stored as a 64-bit count of nanoseconds since 1970-01-01T00:00:00Z, which is numpy’s datetime64[ns]. A variable of date-times can also carry a time zone, which sets the wall-clock time each instant shows: its components (.dt.hour), its resampling bins, how date strings select it, and how it is displayed. The zone never changes the instants themselves, so comparisons, differences and alignment are those of the instants.

import datetime as dt
import zoneinfo

import numpy as np
import whenever
import annoray as ar

Storage#

Date-times are datetime64[ns] and durations timedelta64[ns]: both are 64-bit nanosecond counts in memory, so .values is a read-only view of the variable’s data, not a copy. The range of an instant is that of numpy’s nanoseconds, 1677-09-21 to 2262-04-11. Dates outside it, and dates in the other CF calendars (noleap, 360_day, …), are CF dates (cftime objects in Python). For data read lazily from a file the choice is made when the file is opened, from the first and last stored values, as xarray makes it; a value in between outside the range is an error when the data are read.

times = ar.DataArray(np.array(["2024-05-11T07:00", "2024-05-11T08:00"], "M8[ns]"), dims="t")
a, b = times.values, times.values
print(a.dtype, np.shares_memory(a, b), a.flags.writeable)

datetime64[ns] True False

Times with a zone as input#

Inputs that know their zone are converted to UTC instants exactly, in whole nanoseconds, without numpy’s warning (no explicit representation of timezones available for np.datetime64), and the zone they share becomes the variable’s:

Input

Zone

whenever.ZonedDateTime

its IANA zone

whenever.OffsetDateTime

its offset, such as -04:00

whenever.Instant

none (UTC)

datetime.datetime with a zoneinfo.ZoneInfo or pytz zone

the zone’s name

datetime.datetime with a datetime.timezone

UTC, or the offset

pandas Timestamp, DatetimeIndex or Series with a zone

the zone’s name

 
zoned = [whenever.ZonedDateTime(2024, 5, 11, h, tz="America/New_York") for h in (3, 4)]
da = ar.DataArray(zoned, dims="t")
print(da.values, da.dt.tz)

aware = dt.datetime(2024, 5, 11, 3, tzinfo=zoneinfo.ZoneInfo("Europe/Paris"))
print(ar.DataArray([aware], dims="t").dt.tz)

['2024-05-11T07:00:00.000000000' '2024-05-11T08:00:00.000000000'] America/New_York
Europe/Paris

Times in different zones become UTC instants with no zone. A whenever.PlainDateTime is refused, since it names no instant until a zone is chosen for it (.assume_utc(), .assume_tz(...)); so is a mix of times with and without a zone. Times without a zone (datetime64, naive datetime.datetime) are read as UTC, as in xarray.

try:
    ar.DataArray([whenever.PlainDateTime(2024, 5, 11, 3)], dims="t")
except TypeError as e:
    print(e)

a whenever.PlainDateTime has no time zone; choose one first (.assume_utc(), .assume_tz('Europe/Paris'), .assume_fixed_offset(...))

The zone of a variable#

.dt.tz is the zone’s name: UTC, an offset such as +05:30, or an IANA name such as America/New_York (from the system’s time zone database, or the copy built into annoray where the system has none). None means UTC.

.dt.tz_convert(tz) keeps the instants and changes the zone: only the wall-clock times change.

utc = ar.DataArray(np.array(["2024-07-01T02:00"], "M8[ns]"), dims="t")
ny = utc.dt.tz_convert("America/New_York")
print(ny.dt.hour.values, ny.dt.day.values, (ny.values == utc.values).all())
print(ny)

[22] [30] True
<annoray.DataArray (t: 1)> Size: 8B
[2024-06-30 22:00:00-04:00]
Time zone: America/New_York

.dt.tz_localize(tz) keeps the wall-clock times and changes the instants: times stored as UTC that were in fact local times become the instants those clocks showed. A wall-clock time that the zone skips (clocks going forward) or repeats (clocks going back) is resolved by disambiguate, with whenever’s names: "compatible" (the default: the earlier of two repeated times, a skipped time moved forward by the gap), "earlier", "later" or "raise".

local = ar.DataArray(np.array(["2024-11-03T01:30"], "M8[ns]"), dims="t")
for how in ("earlier", "later"):
    print(how, local.dt.tz_localize("America/New_York", disambiguate=how).values)

earlier ['2024-11-03T05:30:00.000000000']
later ['2024-11-03T06:30:00.000000000']

What the zone changes:

  • Components (.dt.hour, .dt.dayofweek, groupby("time.day"), …) are those of the wall-clock times.

  • Resampling bins fall on wall-clock times: daily bins start at local midnight, so a day across a daylight-saving change lasts 23 or 25 hours. The bins’ labels keep the zone.

  • Date strings in sel are wall-clock times: sel(time="2024-07-01") selects that local day. Labels with a zone (whenever’s, aware datetimes) are instants.

  • Display shows wall-clock times with their offset.

  • Results computed from date-times keep their zone (selection, reductions, a date-time plus a duration); a difference of date-times is a duration, without one.

hours = np.datetime64("2024-07-01T04:00", "ns") + np.arange(48) * np.timedelta64(1, "h")
series = ar.DataArray(np.arange(48.0), dims="time", coords={"time": hours})
local = series.assign_coords(time=series.time.dt.tz_convert("America/New_York"))
print(local.resample(time="D").mean().values)
print(series.resample(time="D").mean().values)
print(local.sel(time="2024-07-02").sizes)

[11.5 35.5]
[ 9.5 31.5 45.5]
{'time': 24}

Date-times in different zones are not combined: concat, merge and alignment of coordinates whose zones differ raise an error asking for one zone (tz_convert), as they do for different units.

Output#

.values stays datetime64[ns] in UTC, a view of the data. .dt.to_whenever() gives whenever objects: ZonedDateTime in an IANA zone, OffsetDateTime at a fixed offset, Instant without a zone, and None for NaT.

print(ny.dt.to_whenever())
print(utc.dt.to_whenever())

[ZonedDateTime("2024-06-30 22:00:00-04:00[America/New_York]")]
[Instant("2024-07-01 02:00:00Z")]

date_range(..., tz=...) returns pandas’ index with its zone (as xarray does); a variable made from it takes the zone.

days = ar.date_range("2024-03-09", periods=3, freq="D", tz="America/New_York")
print(ar.DataArray(days, dims="time").values)

['2024-03-09T05:00:00.000000000' '2024-03-10T05:00:00.000000000'
 '2024-03-11T04:00:00.000000000']

Files#

The zone is written as a time_zone attribute of the variable (CF has no attribute for it; other readers see correct UTC instants) and becomes the variable’s zone again when the file is opened. to_xarray writes the same attribute, and from_xarray (or a time_zone entry in attrs) reads it back.

import tempfile
from pathlib import Path

path = Path(tempfile.mkdtemp()) / "zoned.nc"
ar.Dataset({"v": ("time", [1.0, 2.0])}, coords={"time": zoned}).to_netcdf(path)
back = ar.open_dataset(path)
print(back.time.dt.tz, back.time.attrs)

America/New_York {}
